Steve Jobs (1955–2011)

Steve Jobs was an American entrepreneur, inventor, and visionary who co-founded Apple Inc. and fundamentally transformed the modern digital landscape. Known for his intense perfectionism and focus on elegant product design, he helped spark the personal computer revolution in the late 1970s with the Apple II and the Macintosh. After being forced out of Apple in 1985, he founded NeXT computer and led Pixar Animation Studios, turning it into a powerhouse of digital filmmaking before returning to Apple as CEO in 1997. During his second tenure, he saved the company from near-bankruptcy and ushered in a new era of consumer electronics, spearheading the creation of game-changing innovations like the iPod, iPhone, and iPad. His relentless pursuit of combining technology with the liberal arts reshaped industries from music to cellular communication until his passing from pancreatic cancer in 2011.
